## Design Context

### Users
The primary audience is peers and other technically aware visitors evaluating craft, taste, and front-end capability. The portfolio should help them quickly build trust in the quality of the work, the polish of execution, and the developer's interaction design skills. The experience should feel impressive without becoming confusing or hard to scan.

### Brand Personality
Voice and tone should feel confident, animated, and attractive. The interface should communicate technical fluency, strong motion sensibility, and a clear sense of care in visual execution. The emotional target is confidence first, with bold energy and a strong sense of engagement so the site never feels dull.

### Aesthetic Direction
The current codebase already points toward a modern, technical visual language with glassy surfaces, layered gradients, cyan-violet accents, and spacious card-based sections. The desired direction should evolve this into a more interactive, mouse-reactive experience inspired in part by selected sections from `/Users/ziadsaid/Documents/بروتوفوليو/seif-atef-portfolio`, especially its cinematic motion, cursor-reactive behavior, and high-engagement section treatment. The final result should support both light and dark modes. It must not look generic, overly playful, overly corporate, visually ugly, or tiring to the eye.

### Design Principles
1. Build trust through polish: every section should feel intentional, technically sharp, and carefully composed.
2. Favor high-quality interaction over static presentation: motion, hover behavior, and cursor response should add delight and perceived skill, not noise.
3. Keep the interface visually comfortable: bold energy is welcome, but readability, spacing, and eye comfort always win.
4. Avoid template aesthetics: do not ship layouts, cards, or hero treatments that feel like a generic AI portfolio or a stock landing page.
5. Design for dual themes from the start: light and dark mode should both feel native, not one treated as an afterthought.
